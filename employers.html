Employer guide

The basics for taking on local staff through NearTask, for any kind of business. Checked 8 November 2026.

NearTask only helps you and local people find each other. You are the employer: you agree the pay and hours, do the checks below and pay your staff. NearTask doesn't employ, pay or vet anyone.

2. Right to work checks

  • Check that every new person has the right to work in the UK before they start, whatever their nationality.
  • Keep a dated copy of the check.
  • If you skip it and the person can't legally work, the fine can be up to £45,000 per worker (£60,000 for a repeat).

Source: GOV.UK – Check a job applicant's right to work

3. Sick pay

  • Since 6 April 2026, Statutory Sick Pay is paid from the first day of sickness, with no minimum earnings.
  • It's £123.25 a week, or 80% of the person's average weekly pay if that is lower, for up to 28 weeks.

Source: GOV.UK – Statutory Sick Pay

4. Holiday for shift and casual staff

  • Everyone gets paid holiday, including casual and zero-hours staff.
  • For irregular hours, holiday builds up at 12.07% of the hours worked.
  • For irregular-hours and part-year workers only, you may add holiday pay to each payslip ("rolled-up holiday pay"), shown as a separate line.

Source: GOV.UK – Holiday pay for irregular hours workers

5. Workers aged 16 and 17

  • No more than 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week.
  • A 30-minute break if they work more than 4.5 hours, and 12 hours' rest between working days.
  • Normally no work between 10pm and 6am (or 11pm and 7am if their contract allows work after 10pm). Some jobs, such as hotels and restaurants, have limited exceptions.
  • NearTask lets you mark a shift as adults only.

Source: Acas – Young workers

6. Coming in 2027: shift rules

The Employment Rights Act 2025 plans new rights for zero-hours and low-hours workers, expected in 2027 (details still under consultation):

  • an offer of guaranteed hours that reflect the hours they regularly work;
  • reasonable notice of shifts and of changes;
  • payment when a shift is cancelled, moved or cut short at short notice.

Good habit now: post shifts on NearTask well ahead and keep changes to a minimum.

Source: GOV.UK – Employment Rights Act timeline

7. Paying people

  • If you take someone on as staff, you pay them through your payroll (PAYE) with a payslip.
  • Agree pay, hours and what to bring in the NearTask chat before the shift, so both sides have it in writing.

This is general information to help you get started, not legal advice. Rules can change; check GOV.UK or Acas, or ask an adviser, for your own situation.
